# 05. The goal, the sending stones, and the origin wrinkle

From the 2026-10-11 conversation. Proposals for feedback.

## One reason for the trip, in both timelines: the letter

Earlier draft had two different reasons (a Cryovain weapon in the past, the letter in the future). Simpler and cleaner: **the letter is the reason in both.** Ashe Orendis wrote to his son Kael. Sal received it, as Kael, and could not go. He cannot stand in front of Kael's father wearing Kael's face. So he sent people.

- **Past timeline, with the origin wrinkle:** Sal posted the job in Phandalin and five strangers took it. That is how the party met. Liv ate the trip, so none of them remember it; when they "first met" at the Phandalin job board in the Icespire campaign, it was the second time, and the odd familiarity was real. The Cryovain artifact is not why they went. It is something the island gave them that they stashed and forgot; in the past ending they remember where it is.
- **Future timeline:** the letter arrives after Cryovain. Sal asks his friends to go. Same reason, same ask, Phandalin still needs him.

Sal's reason to stay is the same shape in both: he will not face Ashe, and the town needs him. Only how well he knows the party changes. This also removes the problem of asking the Icespire DM to retcon a dragon attack on Phandalin.

## The obvious goal on the island: bring Ashe home

Leaving the island is not a goal, it is a reward. The goal is **Sal's errand**, which the party does not remember until Sal reminds them.

> "Find Ashe Orendis. Make sure he is alright. Do not tell him anything about me. If he will come, bring him back to Phandalin."

Ashe will not leave. He is waiting for a son who is not coming and for children he cannot remember. To get him off the island the party has to deal with what is holding him there, and what is holding him there is the song, which means Liv, which means Fawkes. The whole mystery is an escort quest with a heart, and the stakes are a friend's secret.

Three pressures on top of it:

1. **They lose themselves every night.** One memory card each time the song plays. Staying costs them who they are. Solving it is the only way to stop the bleed. This is the horror push and it needs no NPC.
2. **The clock.** Remembrance Festival in four days. The weekly ferry leaves the morning after. Fawkes's offer of free passage home expires with the festival.
3. **Sal on the stone.** Three times a day, the one person who remembers the island as it was, asking them to bring an old man home. The DM has a nudge channel in the voice of the person they trust most, and an information source that only ever gives them the broad strokes.

## The sending stones: a live line to the present (Jack, 2026-10-11)

The stone does not reach a Sal who sent them. It reaches **Sal in the Cryovain storyline, as it is happening.** From his side the party is asleep, right there, and the stone is speaking with their voices. He is not separated from them. He can see them breathing. He cannot wake them.

**Sal does not know what is happening.** He can tell them they are asleep beside him, and he will, because he is frightened. That tells the characters their bodies are somewhere else. It does not tell them what the island is, whether it is a memory or a dream or something ahead of them, or how to leave. Nobody on either end knows that. He answers up to three times a day, dream time.

First contact, roughly:

> "Where are you? You are right here. You are all asleep, I am looking at you. What beach? Describe it again."

Then they read him the letter, or say the name on it.

> *silence* "Say the name again." *silence* "Who gave you that?"

Nobody has said Kael to Sal in years. That is the moment Sal knows this is real, whatever it is. And it is the moment he gives them the errand, live, out of guilt, which turns out to be the same errand that was the reason for the trip in whichever timeline this becomes:

> "Listen to me. Find Ashe Orendis. Make sure he is alright. Do not tell him anything about me. If he will come, bring him home."

The loop closes on itself. Sal asks for the thing that already happened, or that is going to.

**What Sal knows that nobody else does.** Kael grew up in Lumenshire. Sal lived every one of Kael's memories. **Sal remembers the island as it was before the rampage**, and he is the only living person who does, because Kael left before Liv happened. On the stone he can describe the town the party is standing in and get it wrong in exactly the ways that matter:

- "The bakery next to Willem's is the Fenwicks'. Marta Fenwick, three kids, the youngest has a lisp." There is a bakery. It is Bartholomew's. He has run it for thirty years and has receipts for customers he never served.
- "Ashe has four children. Kael is the youngest. Mary keeps bees behind the house." The hives are there. Empty.
- "The courthouse is on the little island, you cross the bridge by the pier." There is no bridge.

This is the information engine the campaign was missing. The party does not need the Eternal Tome to learn what was lost; they have a man on a stone who remembers it, three times a day, and every answer he gives them is a hole they can go and stand in. It also solves why they can learn the broad strokes and never the whole: Sal remembers a childhood, not a trial. He left before Elara, before Liv, before Fawkes was anyone.

**Drift.** Each dream session is later in the Cryovain story. Between sessions Sal has been living, and so have they, awake, remembering none of this; only Sal carries the stone conversations forward. Proposed frame: each dream session is one night's sleep in the Icespire game. The song at the end of a dream session is them waking in Phandalin with nothing. Sal wakes next to them knowing. **[Jack to confirm.]** He mentions things the Icespire campaign has done since; the party is hearing their own future from a man who thinks it is the present. Liv's illusions. And across the dream itself the song reaches the stone: static where a name should be, Sal taking a beat too long, and by the fourth day he has to be reminded who is talking.

**For Jack as a player.** Everything Sal hears on the stone, Sal knows in the Icespire game. Jack has an in-character reason to dread a letter he has not received yet, and to behave strangely toward five people he may or may not have met before. The Icespire DM should be told this is happening.

**The sixth.** Sal cannot help with whose blood is on the beach. From where he stands there were five of them when they went down. The sixth stays unsolvable, which is correct.

**Timeline note.** In the past-with-origin version Sal would have received the letter years ago and hired five strangers. Present-day Sal on the stone does not remember doing so. That is either a continuity break (and therefore the future), or the song took it from him too, the way it takes victims from everyone who knew them. Jack decides when the ending is known, not before.
