# 05. The sacrifice, and hearing all three paths

Draft from the 2026-10-11 design conversation. Proposals, not canon until Jack says so.

## The sacrifice: Oblivion needs a keeper

Reframe the spell. Oblivion was never a spell that destroys memories. It **moves** them. The ritual names a keeper, a willing mind that holds what everyone else forgets. Liv, nine years old and casting from her mother's book, skipped the clause she did not understand. With no keeper named, the spell made one: it poured the courtroom's memories into the caster and the caster's body grew to hold them. The hydra is a vessel. Every person she has eaten since is in there.

That gives one rule the whole endgame hangs on:

> **To undo the curse, someone must willingly become the keeper.** Everyone on the island gets their memories back. Liv is a girl again. The keeper is erased from every mind on the island, including the party's, and walks away carrying what nobody else can.

It is Fawkes's current fate, made voluntary and total. He has spent five years as the only one who remembers. The cure asks for the same thing from someone who chooses it.

### Who can pay

- **Fawkes.** The redemption ending. He finally does what he could not do at the courthouse. Liv wakes up and does not know him, and neither does anyone else.
- **A player.** The "player sacrifice" ending. Needs to be earned: the party has to come to care about Liv or the town enough. Tell the players this ending exists at session zero in the abstract ("someone may have to pay for this"), never who.
- **Karleen.** She has already lost everything once and does not know it. Learning the truth and choosing to be forgotten anyway is her arc completing.
- **Ashe.** The dark horse. A man with nothing left to forget volunteers. If the party has been kind to him, this one hurts the most.
- **Seraphina.** Her nightmares mean she already half-holds the truth. A turned Seraphina is a natural keeper.
- **Elara.** Wants it for the opposite reason. Holding every memory on the island is her ascension. Her plan and the cure are the **same ritual with different intent**, which is why the cultists are feeding Liv: fill the vessel first. This answers Q2 (Elara is the will inside the Forgotten, steering toward the ritual) and gives "Elara wins" a mechanism.

### The half measures (why the items matter)

- **Staff of Silence**: stops the song long enough to get close and speak the ritual.
- **Eternal Tome of Lumenshire**: holds the collective knowledge of the town, including the keeper clause that the Oblivion tome leaves out. Reading it is how the party learns the price before paying it.
- **Vial of Complete Restoration**: anchors one mind through the transfer. **Without it the keeper dies. With it the keeper lives, forgotten.** This turns item-hunting into a question of whether the sacrifice is a death or an exile.
- **Killing Liv** stays on the table as the keeper-free route: destroy the vessel and the memories spill back out. Everyone remembers. Liv is dead. Fawkes goes to prison. Nobody has to volunteer, which is exactly why a tired party will take it.

### What Fawkes is actually doing (answers Q3)

The serum is Fawkes trying to be the keeper by force. He has the Oblivion tome but not the Eternal Tome, so he does not know the clause requires consent. His plan: dose the whole town at the Remembrance Festival, pull every memory into himself, die, and leave his daughter human. It would not work. Done without the clause it makes a second vessel, and the Forgotten know it. Team Fawkes players will be running errands for a suicide that would make things worse, and will have to decide whether to tell him.

## Hearing all three paths in the first day

The problem: a party that meets Karleen first and likes her will never hear the other two pitches. Fix: the first day in Lumenshire is structured so that **two hooks come to the party and the third is planted on them.** Each hook is a different theory of the same symptom, and each ends with a concrete ask and a date.

### The clock

The **Remembrance Festival** is in four days. Every hook points at it. Karleen thinks Fawkes is about to do something there. Fawkes needs the potions before it. The cultists call it the Feast. The festival is where the threads converge whether the party chooses or not.

### Morning: Karleen (comes to them)

She finds them in the square with the DON'T FORGET pages. Her theory: **people are vanishing and the mayor knows.** She points at the holes in the shopkeepers. Her ask: "Meet me at the old treehouse past the graveyard at dusk tomorrow. Bring the sword. I think I knew the man who carried it."

### Afternoon: the Vanguard (comes to them)

Seraphina herself, polite and armoured, at the inn. Her theory, which is Fawkes's: **the Forgotten walk among us in disguise, and the town is too soft to see it.** Fawkes's office, the speech, the marbles, the serum errand. His ask: "The potions arrive at Lumen Pier in two days. Bring them to me quietly and you sail home free." Decline and the ask becomes an order.

### Night: the cult (planted on them)

The song plays. Fade to black. In the morning they wake in the inn with cuts they do not remember and **one thing that was not there before**: a cultist's necklace in someone's pocket, or a dead cultist in the alley below their window with a journal (the deleted one, rewritten), or a line of blue chalk symbols on their door. The cult's theory, from the journal: **she is a goddess who takes away pain, and the town is lucky to be fed to her.** The ask is implicit: the journal names the cave on the Reach and the night of the Feast.

Backup pushes if they ignore it: the lost-child search near the cave, Ashe's map in a language they cannot read, Thomas the sailor offering a boat ride past the Reach.

### The routing rule (Jack, 2026-10-11)

1. **Day one: a signal for every path.** All three pitches land before the party sleeps a second night (the morning / afternoon / planted-at-night structure above).
2. **They pursue one.** Whatever bites, run it fully. Do not interrupt it to re-sell the others.
3. **A second path presents itself naturally during the first.** Each track has a built-in handoff to one other track, so the party always finishes their first choice holding a live second option. The handoff is in-fiction, not a DM nudge:
   - Karleen's track hands off to **Fawkes**: breaking into town hall gets them seen; Seraphina summons them, now with leverage. Or, softer: the trial records name a witness the Vanguard is protecting.
   - Fawkes's track hands off to **Karleen**: the first errand is to silence a witness, and the witness is Karleen (or someone carrying her drawings). The marble in their hand is meant for her.
   - The cult track hands off to **Fawkes**: a cultist carries one of Fawkes's marbles, or the captives in the cave are people the Vanguard "arrested". Alternatively to Karleen: her family's names are carved in the cave.
4. **Leave a trail to the third.** Never a pitch, always evidence lying in the path of the other two: blue chalk symbols near the treehouse; a Vanguard patrol roster with a gap on the nights of the feedings; a cultist necklace in Fawkes's desk drawer; Gary mentioning a robed customer who bought all his Mystic Melatonin. The third path is something they can walk toward on their own, and if they never do, the festival brings it to them.

Net effect: every party hears three, chooses one, is handed a second, and can smell the third.

### Letting it bite

After the first day they have heard all three and been given three dates. Let them choose. The two they do not pick keep moving:

- Ignore Karleen: she gets arrested by the Vanguard. Her treehouse is torched. The party hears about it.
- Ignore Fawkes: the Vanguard starts hunting them. Seraphina becomes an antagonist earlier than planned.
- Ignore the cult: disappearances accelerate. A shopkeeper the party talked to is gone and nobody else remembers them.

Each pitch should be delivered by someone sympathetic. Karleen is kind. Seraphina is honourable. The cultist's journal is grief, not villainy. The players should not be able to tell which theory is true from tone alone, because all three are partly right.

## Design constraint: they will only ever get the broad strokes (Jack, 2026-10-11)

Assume the party finishes the campaign knowing part of the story. Design for that instead of against it.

- **Every track must deliver enough to reach an ending on its own.** No ending may require knowledge from all three tracks. The keeper clause, the song-blocking tools, and Fawkes's guilt each need to be learnable from at least two different places.
- **Minimum viable truth per track:**
  - Karleen alone: Fawkes killed his wife, had a daughter, the daughter is the monster. Enough to confront Fawkes or kill Liv. They will not know about the keeper clause unless they find the Eternal Tome.
  - Fawkes alone: he is feeding her and planning something at the festival. Enough to stop him or help him. They may never learn Liv is his daughter until he says so.
  - Cult alone: how the song works, what blocks it, that the Forgotten were people, that "the vessel must be filled". Enough to fight Liv. They may never learn who she is.
- **NPCs fill the gaps in the finale, not the DM.** Fawkes, Liv (through the lair's memory projections), Seraphina's dreams, and the Eternal Tome are the four mouths that can say the truth out loud. Use whichever one the party is standing next to.
- **Unlearned truth is not wasted.** It is the epilogue. Whatever they never found out gets told to them after the ending, as the cost of how they chose.
- **Do not protect the full picture.** If a player guesses the whole thing in session two, let them be right. The horror is not the twist, it is the song.
