# 06. The dream frame (how this campaign plugs into another one)

From Jack, 2026-10-11. This is the book's entry point and is part of the general campaign, not only his home game.

## Premise

Obliviosa is not run as a standalone campaign. It is a **dream campaign**: a DM running some other campaign alternates into Lumenshire intermittently, a session here and there, with the same party. The party arrives with history, levels, gear and relationships already in place. The wreck on the beach is how the dream begins each time it is picked up.

## The one rule the DM tracks

**Characters and inventory persist across dream sessions and across the waking campaign. Nothing is forked.** The DM does not keep a separate Lumenshire sheet. Whatever the character has in the waking game, they have on the island, and whatever they gain or lose on the island, they have when they wake.

This is not a convenience. It is the memory loss made literal. To the players the dream sessions happen weeks apart; to the characters it is all one night on the island. Any continuity conflict this creates (a potion drunk in the waking game that was supposed to be in a pack on the beach, a level gained between visits) is **written off as one of Liv's illusions.** The DM never apologises for it and never explains it. It is the song.

## Past or future

The dream resolves one of two ways, and the party does not know which until it ends:

- **Nobody in the party died in Lumenshire: it was the past.** They wake in the waking campaign remembering all of it, including where they stashed every quest reward on the island. Those caches are real and recoverable. The dream was a memory they had been made to forget, now returned.
- **Any party member died in Lumenshire, or the dream broke continuity with the waking campaign: it was the future.** No recovery. The dreams end for good. The waking campaign runs its course and then arrives at the island for real, where the story picks up where the dream left it.
- **The whole party died: it was only a dream.** Nothing carries either way. (Jack, 2026-10-11.)
- **Erasure by Liv is permadeath**, the same as any other death for this rule.

"Died" means died and stayed dead at the end of the dream. The forgotten friend's erasure happens before the dream begins and never counts. A character brought back inside the dream has not died for this rule.

Tell the players at session zero that the dream is either their past or their future and that they will not know which until it ends. Do not tell them the rule that decides it.

## Consequences for the book

- **Entry requires an existing party.** The book must assume levels, gear and relationships it did not create. Scaling guidance per tier is required instead of a fixed level.
- **The letter beat still works.** The forgotten friend is whoever brought them here. With an existing party the strongest version is a real NPC from the waking campaign who the DM pulls into the dream and feeds to Liv first.
- **Rewards must be stashable.** Every significant reward on the island needs a place it can be left so the past ending can pay out. Design item placement with "where would they hide this" in mind.
- **The keeper sacrifice interacts with the past/future rule.** A keeper is forgotten, not killed. If the party saves Liv through a volunteer and nobody dies, it was the past. The keeper wakes with everyone else; nobody at the table but their own player remembers who they were on the island. (Decided 2026-10-11.)
- **The party does not remember why they came.** That memory is the first thing the song takes. The reason is discovered on the island and should differ by timeline (see any table's integration notes).
- **Session boundaries are the song.** Each time the DM leaves Lumenshire for the waking campaign, the song plays. Each return begins mid-scene or on the beach. Players should expect to lose a memory card at every boundary.

## The void of perception (replaces "blurred faces", Jack, 2026-10-11)

Not blurred faces, and not named characters. **Unnamed people who will die on the island** (guards, citizens, sailors, the body on the beach) are perception voids. The players can interact with them normally, but the person does not register.

Running it:

- The DM never announces it. A player who looks at such an NPC may make a Perception check, or the DM uses passive Perception.
  - Below 10: nothing. The guard is a guard.
  - 10 to 14: the person is extremely forgettable. The player cannot describe them a minute later.
  - 15 to 19: something is off. The player knows they are looking at someone and cannot say what they look like.
  - 20 or more: the face will not resolve. There is a person-shaped gap where the detail should be.
- Named NPCs are shown normally, whether or not they are going to die. The rule is about the nameless. Naming someone at the table is a small act of mercy and the players may work that out.
- If a PC dies in the dream, their portrait in later recaps is treated the same way. This is the first visible sign the dream is the future.
- Killing Liv restores the faces. Describe one of the nameless dead clearly in the epilogue.
- The in-fiction reason: the dream is one moment; the players' minds cannot hold a person whose erasure has already happened further down the night.
