# 05. The goal, the sending stones, and the origin wrinkle

From the 2026-10-11 conversation. Proposals for feedback.

## One reason for the trip, in both timelines: the letter

Earlier draft had two different reasons (a Cryovain weapon in the past, the letter in the future). Simpler and cleaner: **the letter is the reason in both.** Ashe Orendis wrote to his son Kael. Sal received it, as Kael, and could not go. He cannot stand in front of Kael's father wearing Kael's face. So he sent people.

- **Past timeline, with the origin wrinkle:** Sal posted the job in Phandalin and five strangers took it. That is how the party met. Liv ate the trip, so none of them remember it; when they "first met" at the Phandalin job board in the Icespire campaign, it was the second time, and the odd familiarity was real. The Cryovain artifact is not why they went. It is something the island gave them that they stashed and forgot; in the past ending they remember where it is.
- **Future timeline:** the letter arrives after Cryovain. Sal asks his friends to go. Same reason, same ask, Phandalin still needs him.

Sal's reason to stay is the same shape in both: he will not face Ashe, and the town needs him. Only how well he knows the party changes. This also removes the problem of asking the Icespire DM to retcon a dragon attack on Phandalin.

## The obvious goal on the island: bring Ashe home

Leaving the island is not a goal, it is a reward. The goal is **Sal's errand**, which the party does not remember until Sal reminds them.

> "Find Ashe Orendis. Make sure he is alright. Do not tell him anything about me. If he will come, bring him back to Phandalin."

Ashe will not leave. He is waiting for a son who is not coming and for children he cannot remember. To get him off the island the party has to deal with what is holding him there, and what is holding him there is the song, which means Liv, which means Fawkes. The whole mystery is an escort quest with a heart, and the stakes are a friend's secret.

Three pressures on top of it:

1. **They lose themselves every night.** One memory card each time the song plays. Staying costs them who they are. Solving it is the only way to stop the bleed. This is the horror push and it needs no NPC.
2. **The clock.** Remembrance Festival in four days. The weekly ferry leaves the morning after. Fawkes's offer of free passage home expires with the festival.
3. **Sal on the stone.** Three times a day, a voice that remembers what they were sent to do. The DM has a built-in nudge channel in the voice of the person they trust most.

## The sending stones: Sal answers, three times a day

Jack's call: the party's sending stone reaches Sal, and he answers up to three times per day.

What this does to the Sal-is-dead misdirection: it ends the moment they use the stone, and what replaces it is better. They hear Sal alive. Relief. Then:

> "What do you mean who else? There were five of you when you left."

They count five. They look at the blood. Whose was it? Sal does not know, cannot know, and the perception void on the beach means they will never fully know either. The mystery of the sixth replaces the grief for Sal, and it is a mystery nobody can solve, which is the point.

Rules for Sal on the stone:

- He knows the job and the letter. He will repeat the ask. He will not explain Kael unless asked directly, and then only part of it ("he was someone I knew, his father does not know he is gone, please do not be the one who tells him").
- He is in Phandalin time. His answers drift against the dream: he mentions things that have not happened yet for them, or that happened differently. Liv's illusions. Never explained.
- Each use after the first in a day costs something small: static where a word should be, or Sal taking a beat too long to remember a name. By the fourth day he has to be reminded who is talking.
- Three uses means the DM can be stingy on the island and generous on the way out. The third use on the last night is Sal asking whether Ashe is coming home.
